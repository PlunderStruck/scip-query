## Accepted results

- Incremental logical-output accounting fell from 90 MiB to 30.25 MiB, a 66.4% reduction, while the full-rebuild result remained 90 MiB.
- Warm exact source-search median fell from 1,085.670 ms to 680.398 ms, a 37.3% reduction. Maximum observed RSS fell from 1,873,641,472 to 554,418,176 bytes, a 70.4% reduction, despite the final working tree containing more scanned bytes and matches.
- Warm graph-literal median fell from 193.958 ms to 189.191 ms, a 2.5% reduction. Maximum observed RSS fell from 327,876,608 to 312,295,424 bytes, a 4.8% reduction, with an identical result hash.
- Within one generation-pinned database, caching graph structural inputs lowered the controlled 15-run median from 197.964 ms to 187.567 ms (5.3%) and maximum observed RSS from 319,668,224 to 294,273,024 bytes (7.9%). First-call latency also fell 1.9%; the result hash was identical.
- Across three unchanged-tree replicates at 32 concurrent compact result-only searches, trusted-watcher automatic mode produced median paired reductions of 37.0% in aligned peak RSS, 7.0% in wall time, and 32.1% in median completion. At eight workers it exchanged 23.1% more wall time and 3.2% higher median completion for 14.1% less peak RSS; one worker was within 1.5% wall time and 5.4% peak RSS. Every compared result was byte-identical. Single-lane, two-lane, eager full-CLI, unverified-default, and four-lane variants remain recorded as rejected or superseded controls.
- Reusing one 1 MiB streaming probe buffer per scan lowered warm source-search RSS from 540,737,536 to 210,436,096 bytes (61.1%) while changing the median from 315.712 to 315.337 ms (-0.1%). In the six-lane service, median aligned peak RSS fell 53.4% at one client, 47.8% at eight, and 27.6% at 32; median wall time improved 9.5%, 6.6%, and 7.1%. At 32 clients, server-only peak RSS fell 68.3% and median client completion improved 12.4%. Direct and service hashes were byte-identical.
- Extending the same lanes to compact result-only outline requests improved median paired wall time by 30.8% at one client, 28.7% at eight, and 35.8% at 32; median completion improved 32.5%, 33.5%, and 36.7%. At 32 clients, aligned peak RSS fell 9.6%; at eight it rose 4.4%. The fully warm one-client measurement includes the retained six-lane pool rather than incremental outline memory. Every direct/service output hash was identical.
- Extending the lanes to compact result-only code requests improved median paired wall time by 34.3% at one client, 36.4% at eight, and 38.5% at 32; median completion improved 36.0%, 39.6%, and 37.1%. At 32 clients aligned peak RSS fell 11.2%; at eight it rose 10.7%. The first cold lane cost 7.1% wall time and 6.1% aligned RSS, while the fully warm one-client total includes all six retained lanes and was 292.5% above a standalone process. The code module is lazy, no additional lanes are created, and every direct/service output hash was identical.
- Extending the lanes to compact result-only entrypoint discovery improved median paired wall time by 64.5% at one client, 70.2% at eight, and 69.8% at 32 on a non-empty filtered result; median completion improved 65.7%, 76.0%, and 76.7%. At eight clients aligned peak RSS fell 14.0%, and at 32 it fell 49.5%. The first cold lane cost 16.5% wall time and 6.0% RSS; the fully warm one-client total includes all six retained lanes and was 353.9% above a standalone process until the idle timeout releases them. Empty-filter, non-empty, scoped, and complete direct/service bytes were identical.
- Extending the lanes to compact result-only file listing improved median paired wall time by 26.7% at one client, 35.3% at eight, and 30.5% at 32; median completion improved 28.0%, 43.0%, and 25.4%. At 32 clients aligned peak RSS fell 4.5%; at eight it rose 5.9%. Statistics improved median paired wall time by 32.7%, 31.3%, and 41.9%, while median completion improved 34.3%, 36.7%, and 39.6%. At 32 clients statistics RSS fell 5.6%; at eight it rose 12.1%. The warm one-client measurements include all six retained lanes. File lists are recomputed from the live workspace on every service request, and outputs that require the canonical pagination warning fall through to the full CLI. Direct/service stdout, stderr, exit status, and hashes were identical for exact, glob, oversized, and dash-prefixed file patterns and current-generation statistics.
- Extending the lanes to compact result-only member queries improved median paired wall time by 25.0% at one client, 12.7% at eight, and 28.8% at 32; median completion improved 26.5%, 30.4%, and 26.0%. Aligned peak RSS fell 6.2% at eight and 6.8% at 32. Method queries improved median paired wall time by 34.5%, 15.9%, and 33.4%, while median completion improved 35.7%, 33.8%, and 30.7%; aligned peak RSS fell 4.4% at 32 and rose 3.8% at eight. The fully warm one-client total includes all six retained lanes and was 261.5% above a standalone member process and 230.9% above a standalone method process. The first cold one-client lane cost 15.7% for members and 1.5% for methods, while eight- and 32-client cold runs improved. Matched, missing, and ambiguous stdout, stderr, exit status, and hashes were identical; missing and ambiguous method results still exit nonzero.
- Extending the lanes to compact result-only dependency queries improved 32-worker median paired wall time by 40.8% for forward dependencies and 35.8% for reverse dependencies; median completion improved 36.9% and 32.6%, while aligned peak RSS fell 12.2% and 8.4%. Forward queries were 1.1% faster at one client and 17.9% faster at eight, although eight-client RSS rose 13.4%. Reverse queries regressed 8.9% at one client and 4.5% at eight, with 3.2% lower and 6.9% higher RSS respectively. A one- through six-lane sweep found no smaller count that reliably improved both eight- and 32-worker latency, so the shared six-lane policy remains. Exact and missing results were byte-identical; a 35 KB reverse-dependency result fell through to the canonical CLI and preserved its pagination warning. The forward benchmark hash changed only because the implementation added a real type dependency to the measured file; direct and service hashes remained identical on each generation.
- A four-lane cap for compact catalog/navigation queries improved 32-worker median paired wall time by 28.5% for `imported-by`, 17.8% for `hierarchy`, 22.8% for `by-kind`, and 16.2% for `kind-counts`; median completion improved 25.6%, 12.4%, 19.0%, and 14.5%, while aligned peak RSS fell 6.4%, 7.5%, 6.3%, and 6.5%. At eight clients, `hierarchy`, `by-kind`, and `kind-counts` wall time improved 35.8%, 38.4%, and 8.0%; `imported-by` regressed 9.6% against an unusually fast current direct sample while using 18.3% less RSS. `kind-counts` RSS rose 15.6% at eight clients. Fully warm one-client totals include retained catalog lanes and therefore are not incremental per-query memory measurements. A two- through six-lane sweep selected four lanes over the faster-at-32 but much larger six-lane footprint. Exact and missing direct/service stdout, stderr, and exit status were identical, and unsupported scopes, limits, full output, or pagination fall through to the canonical CLI.
- A five-lane semantic-navigation cap improved median paired reference-query wall time by 42.3% at one client, 40.4% at eight, and 41.4% at 32; median completion improved 43.8%, 45.3%, and 41.6%. Aligned peak RSS fell 21.2% at eight clients and 25.5% at 32, while the fully warm one-client total retained five initialized lanes and was 177.0% above a standalone process. Import-query wall time improved 29.6%, 31.9%, and 28.5%; completion improved 32.3%, 35.3%, and 26.1%. Import RSS rose 11.1% at eight clients and was effectively flat at 32 (-0.3%). Repeated four-, five-, and six-lane controls selected five: compared with six, it kept eight-client throughput slightly better and reduced retained server RSS by roughly 12-21%, in exchange for roughly 6-8% slower 32-client throughput. The first reference implementation omitted canonical pagination metadata and failed byte identity; removing and reapplying the correction reproduced and eliminated the mismatch. Exact, missing, full, and oversized direct/service stdout, stderr, and exit status were identical after the fix.
- Extending the existing six lanes to compact result-only consumer-surface queries improved median paired wall time by 4.8% at eight clients and 28.3% at 32; median completion improved 6.1% and 24.8%, while aligned peak RSS rose 9.8% at eight and fell 1.5% at 32. The fully warm one-client measurement regressed 1.6% wall time and retained 71.5% more aligned RSS than a standalone process. Three- through six-lane controls kept six because smaller pools reduced 32-client throughput without a reliable aligned-RSS win. Exact, missing, and broad direct/service stdout, stderr, exit status, and hashes were identical. The paired system-summary experiment was rejected after two repetitions: at 32 clients it was 30-37% slower and used 20-35% more aligned peak RSS because the representative 570 KB result exceeded the 32 KB unpaged bound, causing both service computation and full-CLI pagination. `system` remains explicitly ineligible for the fast path.
